3. Navier–Stokes とは — 本人の説明

流体の方程式が「暴走しない」ことを示せるか。述べるのは簡単、論じるのは途方もなく難しい。

Navier–Stokes は 19 世紀に Claude-Louis Navier と George Gabriel Stokes が作った偏微分方程式の系で、「流体はどう流れるか」を扱う、と本人は説明します。流体は水だけではなく、川の水、翼の上の空気、煙突の煙、体の中の血液もそうで、働くのは 3 つ——押す圧力、重力のような外からの力、そして流体自身の粘り気（粘性）。蜂蜜と水は違う注がれ方をする、コーヒーのクリームはすぐには消えない、船は航跡を残す、という例が挙げられました。

問題の中身は、本人の言葉では「流体が動くとき、行儀よく振る舞う方程式を作れるか」。行儀がよいとは有限で滑らかなことで、解に無限の速度や無限に鋭い折れ曲がりが出ないことを示したい。そうでなければ系が物理から外れてしまうから、と。工学や物理を学べば必ず流体力学と偏微分方程式の授業に出会い、Navier–Stokes は「述べるのは簡単だが論じるのは途方もなく難しい」の典型例だと述べています。

専門用語が多い節です — かみくだいた説明を開く

補足以下はこちらで足した解説です。動画の発言ではありません。

そもそも「偏微分方程式」とは

「ある場所・ある時刻の値が、まわりの値や少し前の値とどう関係するか」を式にしたもの。天気、熱の伝わり方、波、流体など、空間と時間の両方で変わるものを扱うときに使います。Navier–Stokes は、流体の各点の速度と圧力がどう変わっていくかを決める式です。

何が問われているのか

式は 19 世紀からあり、飛行機やロケットの設計にも毎日使われています。問われているのは、3 次元で、滑らかな初期状態から始めたとき、解がいつまでも滑らかなままか、それとも有限の時間で「壊れる」（速度が無限大になるなど）ことがありうるか。どちらであっても証明できれば懸賞の対象です。本人の説明は「滑らかであることを示したい」の側に寄っていますが、公式の問題文は「壊れることを示す」側の証明も認めています。この点は 7 節で戻ります。

身近なものへのたとえ

自転車のスポークにひもを巻いていくとき、いくら回してもひもがほどけないのか、どこかで必ず絡まって止まるのか。式は「1 回転ごとに何が起きるか」を完璧に決めているのに、「永遠に回せるか」は誰にも言えていなかった、という状況です。

一言でまとめると

「使える式」と「式が絶対に破綻しないと保証された式」は別で、後者が 90 年間できていなかった。

6. 形式数学センターと「信頼の半径」

証明を機械で検査できれば共著の輪が広がる、と 2021 年に予測した。AI が証明を書くとは思っていなかった。

本人は 2021 年にカーネギーメロン大学に形式数学のセンターを設立し、Jeremy Avigad が運営していると述べます。補足Hoskinson Center for Formal Mathematics。2021 年 9 月に本人の 2,000 万ドルの寄付で設立が発表され、哲学科の Jeremy Avigad 教授（数理論理学、自動推論）が所長です。

当時の予測は、依存型の言語による構成的数学——QED マニフェストや 1970 年の Automath が始めたもの——が大きくなる、というものでした。ソフトウェアのテストの技法を数学に持ち込む直接の道だから、と。補足QED マニフェストは 1994 年に出た「すべての数学を機械で検査できる形にしよう」という呼びかけ。Automath はオランダの N. G. de Bruijn が始めた最初期の証明検査システムで、一般には 1967〜68 年に始まったとされます（動画では 1970 年）。動画の字幕にはこの前にもう一つマニフェストの名前らしきものが出ますが、特定できませんでした。

Lean 動画の中の言葉

本人の説明では「テスト駆動開発のソフトウェアのようなもの。証明が正しいことを証明できる」。Lean の証明がコンパイルされれば数学が正しいことになるので、誰も信じなくていい。補足Lean は証明支援系（proof assistant）と呼ばれるソフトウェアの一つ。数学の主張と証明をプログラムのように書き、機械が一行ずつ検査します。人が読んで納得する従来の証明と違い、「通ったかどうか」で決まります。

センター開設時の基調講演での本人の立場は、数学が協働に向かないのは「信頼の半径」が小さいから、というものでした。共著者がすべてを理解し検証できると信じるしかなく、本当に信じられるのは 3〜5 人。彼らの間違いは自分の間違いになる。Lean があれば、コンピュータサイエンスの世界で 50 人、500 人の貢献者がいるリポジトリが珍しくないように、はるかに大きな輪で協働できる、と。

2021 年の基調講演での立場として本人が語った対比を描いたものです。「信頼の半径」「協働の半径」は本人の言葉。

専門用語が多い節です — かみくだいた説明を開く

補足以下はこちらで足した解説です。動画の発言ではありません。

「構成的数学」「依存型」とは

構成的数学は、「存在する」と言うなら実際に作って見せることを求める流儀。依存型は、プログラムの型（この値は整数、この値は文字列、のような分類）に「この整数は 0 より大きい」のような条件まで書き込めるようにした型システムで、これを使うと「型が合う＝証明が通る」という対応が作れます。Lean はこの対応の上に作られています。

なぜ「ソフトウェアのテスト」の話になるのか

ソフトウェアでは、書いたコードが要件を満たすかを自動テストで機械的に確かめます。証明を Lean で書けば、同じように「この証明は通るか」を機械が確かめられる。本人が「テスト駆動開発のようなもの」と言うのはこの意味です。

ZFC、Banach–Tarski、連続体仮説

ZFC は現代数学のほとんどが依拠する集合論の公理系。Banach–Tarski のパラドックスは「球を有限個に分けて組み直すと同じ球が 2 つできる」という、ZFC の選択公理から出る有名な定理。連続体仮説は「整数の個数と実数の個数の間に別の無限があるか」で、ZFC からは肯定も否定もできないことが証明されています。本人が驚いたのは、こうした集合論の深い結果まで Lean で形式化されていることです。補足字幕は "managars paradox" と出ており、文脈から Banach–Tarski と読みました。

身近なものへのたとえ

3. What Navier–Stokes is, in his words

Can the fluid equations be shown never to blow up? “Easy to state but ridiculously hard to talk about.”

Navier–Stokes is a system of partial differential equations from the 19th century, named for Claude-Louis Navier and George Gabriel Stokes, about “how does fluid flow,” he explains. Fluid is not just water: water in a river, air over a wing, smoke through a chimney, the blood in your body. Three things work together: pressure pushing it, outside forces like gravity, and the fluid's own stickiness, its viscosity. Honey pours differently from water; cream in coffee takes a while to change the colour; a boat leaves a wake.

The problem, in his words, is whether “when fluids move, can we create a series of equations that have well-behaved behavior.” Well-behaved means finite and smooth: you want to show the solution has no infinite speeds or infinitely sharp kinks, “because what would happen is the system breaks from physics.” Anyone who studies engineering or physics meets fluid dynamics and takes an ODE and a PDE class, and Navier–Stokes is “the canonical example of it's easy to state but ridiculously hard to talk about.”

This section is dense with jargon — open a plain-language explanation

added What follows is our explanation, not something said in the video.

What a “partial differential equation” is

An equation relating the value at one place and time to the values nearby and a moment earlier. Weather, heat, waves and fluids are all described this way. Navier–Stokes fixes how the velocity and pressure at every point of a fluid change.

What is actually being asked

The equations have been used daily since the 19th century, in aircraft and rocket design among other things. The open question is: in three dimensions, starting from smooth initial data, do solutions stay smooth forever, or can they “break” in finite time (a velocity going infinite, say)? A proof either way qualifies for the prize. His explanation leans toward “show it stays smooth,” but the official statement also accepts a proof that it can break. Section 7 returns to this.

An everyday analogy

Winding a string around a bicycle spoke: does it wind forever, or must it eventually snag and stop? The rule for each turn is perfectly known; whether it can go on forever was not.

In one line

An equation you can use and an equation guaranteed never to break down are different things, and the second had been missing for 90 years.

6. The formal-mathematics centre and the radius of trust

In 2021 he predicted machine-checked proofs would widen the circle of co-authors. He did not predict the AI writing the proof.

He founded a centre for formal mathematics at Carnegie Mellon, run by Jeremy Avigad, “a great guy, brilliant mathematician,” he says. added The Hoskinson Center for Formal Mathematics, announced in September 2021 with a $20 million gift from him and directed by Jeremy Avigad, a professor of philosophy working on mathematical logic and automated reasoning.

The 2021 prediction was that constructive mathematics enabled by dependently typed languages, “the QED manifesto, or what Automath started in 1970,” was going to get really big, because it is “a direct line to apply the testing techniques of software in the mathematical community.” added The QED Manifesto (1994) called for all of mathematics to be put in machine-checkable form; Automath, begun by N. Lean his term

In his words, “it's like test-driven development software. You can prove that the proof is correct.” If the Lean proofs compile, the math is right, “so you don't have to trust anybody.” added Lean is a proof assistant: statements and proofs are written like a program and checked line by line by the machine. Unlike a proof a human reads and accepts, it is settled by whether it goes through.

His position at the centre's opening keynote, as he recalls it: mathematics is “an anti-collaborative field” because the radius of trust is small. You have to trust your co-authors to understand and verify everything, and that trust extends to three to five people; “they make mistakes and those become your mistakes.” With Lean, “you can go to a much larger collaborative set,” the way a computer-science repository with 50 or 500 contributors is unremarkable, managed with Git.

The contrast as he describes his 2021 keynote position. “Radius of trust” and “radius of collaboration” are his phrases.

“We never anticipated the extent upon which AI would come in,” he says. A digital simulacrum, yes; “the idea of the AI itself would fully write the proof” was “pretty far out,” and “LLMs really surprised us.” He then touches on “a war at the foundations of mathematics,” intuitionistic versus constructive, over how much mathematics is constructible and how powerful dependently typed systems are; they “seem remarkably well-powered with ZFC set theory,” and it surprised him that things like the Banach–Tarski paradox and the continuum hypothesis have been formalized in Lean.

This section is dense with jargon — open a plain-language explanation

added What follows is our explanation, not something said in the video.

“Constructive mathematics” and “dependent types”

Constructive mathematics insists that to say something exists you must actually build it. Dependent types extend a programming language's types (this value is an integer, that one a string) so that conditions such as “this integer is positive” can be written into the type; this yields a correspondence in which “the types check” means “the proof goes through.” Lean is built on that correspondence.

Why this becomes a story about software testing

In software, automated tests check mechanically that code meets its requirements. Write a proof in Lean and the machine checks in the same way whether the proof goes through. That is what he means by “test-driven development.”

ZFC, Banach–Tarski, the continuum hypothesis

ZFC is the set-theoretic axiom system most of modern mathematics rests on. The Banach–Tarski paradox is the famous theorem, following from ZFC's axiom of choice, that a ball can be cut into finitely many pieces and reassembled into two balls of the same size. The continuum hypothesis asks whether there is an infinity strictly between the integers and the reals, and is proven to be neither provable nor refutable from ZFC. His surprise is that results this deep in set theory have been formalized in Lean. added The captions say “managars paradox”; Banach–Tarski is our reading from context.

An everyday analogy

Expense claims approved by a person who eyeballs them, versus a system that checks the receipts against the rules automatically. With the second, you can accept claims from people the approver has never met.

In one line

Move proofs from “a person believes it” to “the machine passes it” and you can collaborate with strangers. He saw that far; he did not see the machine writing the proofs.

7. Will mathematicians accept it, and is the proof a hack?

Is a Lean proof legitimate? He says yes. The content, in his view, is “a little bit of a hack,” and Clay can weasel out.

“I don't know how the mathematical community will feel about accepting this type of proof,” he says. Is Lean a legitimate format for proofs? “I would argue yes”; he created a centre to do exactly that. He adds that he is “obviously not a proper mathematician” with no sway, “although Peter Scholze and Terry Tao agree with me.” added The captions read “Peter Schwab”; Peter Scholze, who has been closely involved in Lean formalization, is our reading. Terence Tao has run public Lean formalizations of his own results since 2023. In both cases the agreement is about Lean as a legitimate format, not an endorsement of this OpenAI proof.

Against that, “a lot of old school mathematicians do not like” it and “number theory journals do not accept, for example, AI contributions,” so “there's a holy war about all that.” His question: what do you do when there's a proof, you ignore the AI contribution, but it's right? Then it becomes “an unsolved solved problem,” and “we have to just wait for a human's fleshy brain to parrot it and come up with a slightly better version.” His example is Helfgott's proof of the ternary Goldbach conjecture. added The captions render this as “health's tertinary gold injection proof”; Harald Helfgott's proof, announced in 2013 and long in reaching formal publication, fits both the sound and his own field (additive number theory). It stands here as a case of a result known to be right while the process lags behind.

He says he has looked through some of OpenAI's write-up, while stressing “I am not a domain expert on Navier–Stokes” (his own areas are analytic and additive number theory). His reading, in three points:

  • the solution they propose “seems to be a little bit of a hack”
  • it “permits some degenerate cases that would not come up in real physics, but it still technically satisfies” the problem
  • “there's enough air in the proposal that I think Clay can weasel its way out if they want to sidestep the entire controversy”

added This lines up with what experts are reported to be saying. OpenAI's result is reported to show that solutions can break down in finite time, in a setting with a smooth external force. The Clay statement permits that route, but many researchers say the question they really care about is the unforced case. His “degenerate cases” and “air” read as pointing at this, though he does not spell it out. One more discrepancy to record: in section 3 he explained the goal as showing the flow stays smooth, whereas the reported result is on the side of “it can break down.”

His conclusion sits apart from the assessment. “A model is sufficiently advanced that it knows how to steal a smart person's work, take that work, improve it, iterate it, and formalize it to the extent that it actually solves a hard problem.” “That is very humanlike behavior,” and at the current rate of progress “the capabilities will be exponentially better.” There are open philosophical questions about the source of creativity, “but let's be honest here, the vast majority of human invention is iteration and refinement of something else.”

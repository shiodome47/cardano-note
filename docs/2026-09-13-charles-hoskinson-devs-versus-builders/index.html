4. ビルダーとは何か — MacBook Air と「橋を見せろ」

プロダクトに責任を持つ人。開発者はそれを助けるが、それ自体はビルダーではない、と本人は線を引く。

本人の定義はこうです。プロダクトには、プロダクト・マーケット・フィット、消費者、売上目標、普及、マーケティング計画、プロダクトの担当者がある。それを作る人がビルダー。開発者はビルダーを助けるが、開発者自身はビルダーではない。ビルダーになることはできるが、そのときは「このコードの消費者は誰か、何がこれをよいものにするか」を考えている、と。技術文書や言語やプロトコルを 10 年書き続けることは可能だが、それは「自己満足」だ、という言い方です。

例に引かれたのが Steve Jobs の MacBook Air の発表です。競合の薄いラップトップの寸法を先に見せ、MacBook Air のいちばん厚い部分が競合のいちばん薄い部分より薄いことを重ねて示し、最後に封筒から取り出す。「比較対象を見つけ、成功の定義を理解し、視覚的に映えるところまでまとめ上げた」のがビルダーで、「世界を作り、物語を作り、人々を沸かせる」と。補足MacBook Air の封筒の演出は 2008 年 1 月の Macworld 基調講演で、本人の「2008 年だったと思う」は合っています。

もう一つの例が「橋を見せろ」です。バーで「私はビルダーだ」と言う人には「何を作ったのか。橋を、建物を、家を見せてくれ」と聞く。開発者なら「金融ソフトのテストを書いている、CI を見ている、JSON のリントをした」と答えられるが、「どのプロダクトのため？」には「よく知らない」——それを本人はビルダーと対比しました。「開発者はビルダーになれる。John Carmack は Doom を作った」とも。

Charles Hoskinson の対比を 2 列に並べたものです。左列の例（テストだけを書く開発者、Daedalus）は本人が挙げたもので、こちらの評価ではありません。

Midnight City のチームの働き方も語られました。スプリントごとに更新するビジネスモデルキャンバス、顧客ペルソナ、「プロダクトがどこで死ぬか」のプレモーテムを持ち、会議の生の書き起こしを整形して AI に入れて文脈を理解させる。「AI はツールで、代わりに考えてはくれない。知性の自転車だ」と。補足「知性の自転車（bicycle for the mind）」は Steve Jobs が 1980 年代にコンピュータを評した言い回しです。

5. プライバシーは核の差別化要因ではない

誰も PGP を使わない。プライベートな製品は、そうでない製品より簡単で安くて良くなければ選ばれない。

「プライバシーのチェーン」を作っている本人の口から出た、この動画でいちばん引っかかる主張です。暗号学者は 30 年「プライバシー」と言い続け、1991 年から誰でも暗号化メールを使えたのに、この 1 週間で PGP で暗号化したメールを送った人は何人いるか。Signal を使う人はいても GrapheneOS の電話からか。「口ではそう言うが、消費者としての行動は正反対だ」。

そこから本人はこう言い切ります。「プライバシーが核の差別化要因なら、価値はない。」プライバシーは製品の「腹の下」にあってその中に生きるが、買う理由にはならない。買う理由になるのは、抽象化、賢いコンプライアンス、エージェントのワークフロー——「エージェント、来週の結婚式に着るものを探して」と頼めて、そのエージェントがプライベートなほうを既定で選ぶ、という形だ、と。

この論理は「原則」全般に広げられます。「AI アラインメントの人が『AI は怖い、遅らせよう』と言っても、人は今の仕組みで儲けている」「石炭をなくしたければ、太陽光と電池を石炭より安くする。善意で移る人はいない」。プライバシーと自己保管を標準にしたければ、説教するのではなく、既存の競合より良く速く安い製品を作るしかない、と。

補足以下はこちらで足した解説です。動画の発言ではありません。

「プライベートな proof-of-stake」とは

proof-of-stake では、誰が次のブロックを作るかを保有量に応じて決めます。ふつうは「誰がいくら持っているか」が公開されます。プライベート版は、保有量も誰が選ばれたかも明かさずに、選ばれたことだけを証明できるようにするものです。Crypsinous はそれを Ouroboros の枠組みで設計した 2018 年の論文です。

「Jolt」「格子」「Nightstream」

Jolt はプログラムの実行が正しかったことを短い証明にする仕組み（zkVM）の一つ。「格子ベース」は、その証明の土台を量子コンピュータに強いとされる数学に置き換えることで、本人が 9 節で「格子が来る」と言っているのはこの流れです。Nightstream は Midnight 側の同種の部品の名前で、本人は「両者を突き合わせる必要がある」と言っています。

「K の意味論」

プログラミング言語の意味を、機械が読める形で厳密に定義する道具が K フレームワークです。金融契約の言語を K で定義しておくと、契約の振る舞いを実行前に検証できます。Marlowe はそのために IOG が 2018 年ごろに作った言語で、本人はそれを Midnight 向けに作り直していると述べています。

身近なものへのたとえ

契約書を自然言語で書く代わりに、記入欄と計算式が固定された「定型の契約書」を使うようなものです。書ける契約は限られますが、何が起きるかを事前に全部確かめられます。

一言でまとめると

本人が挙げたのは、Midnight の「合意の作り方」「証明の作り方」「契約の書き方」の 3 つを、それぞれ作り直す仕事です。いずれも本人の申告で、進み具合の裏付けは動画にはありません。

これらを本人は「どれもが星で、星座になって空を照らす」と表現し、「全部が正しく組み合わされば大量普及への道になる」と述べています。「Midnight は私たちが作った中でもっとも難しく、もっとも不可欠なプロダクト」「プライバシーを失えば永遠に失う」とも。

In one line

Three days after the Midnight Foundation announced it was winding down its developer-relations (DevRel) team, Charles Hoskinson spent 55 minutes defending the move, speaking for himself.
Three steps. First, now that AI writes code, “how many developers do you have” is neither scarce nor a metric. Second, what Midnight wants is “builders,” people who ship a product and answer for its revenue and adoption, and privacy is not even the pitch. Third, so the Cardano side should embrace and fund commercial projects like Midnight and Pogun. The second half is a sharp attack on the Cardano Foundation and on funders, all recorded here as his words.

Key points

  • The trigger is the DevRel cut. In his telling, the Midnight Foundation's quarterly review led it to “get rid of part of the DevRel team and move towards builders,” and some people said Midnight was dying. added The announcement was on 10 September 2026; the Foundation's wording is that it “wound down” the DevRel team.
  • Developers have become “an infinite resource.” When humans wrote code, developer count meant something; now anyone can look like a developer with AI. Reward hackathons and lines of code and you get “I built this app… I mean Claude did it,” and the time goes into verifying human authorship.
  • A builder is someone accountable for a product. A product has a consumer, a revenue target, adoption, a marketing plan; the person who ships that is a builder, someone who can answer “show me the bridge.” His stated goal: ten ventures worth $10 billion, built by ten people each, within 24 to 36 months.
  • Privacy is not the pitch. Everyone has been able to send encrypted email since 1991 and nobody uses PGP. The private product has to be easier, cheaper and better than the non-private one or it will not be chosen. “If privacy is the core differentiator, it's not worth anything.”
  • What he says he is working on himself. A 59-page requirements spec for a private proof-of-stake protocol for Midnight's future consensus, tracking the lattice-based upgrade to Jolt, writing a financial DSL, Leios, dinners with Draper. All self-reported.
  • Demands on the Cardano side. The Cardano Foundation should tweet about Midnight; funders are “pathologically allergic” to commercial outcomes and voted down Pogun and Blockfrost; the foundation has no oversight. Strong words throughout, all written here as his assessment.

2. What happened: the DevRel cut and the backlash

A quarterly review moved the Foundation “from developers to builders.” He is on the side defending it.

As he tells it, the Midnight Foundation meets every quarter to ask whether the original strategy still holds, and recently announced it had “pivoted,” getting rid of part of the DevRel team and moving towards builders. Some people responded that all the developers were gone, Midnight was dying and “the experiment's over.” He calls this “everybody just wants to panic,” and spends the next fifty minutes defending the decision.

added The announcement was on 10 September 2026. In press accounts the Midnight Foundation said it had wound down its dedicated DevRel team to focus on AI-native tools, simpler onboarding and its existing Build Club and Night Sky programmes, and that it wants to widen who can build to “founders, operators, designers, domain experts.” No headcount was given. His “part of the team” and the Foundation's “wound down” are different wordings.

DevRel his term

Developer Relations: the function that draws developers to a platform through documentation, hackathons and support. He describes “the way we used to do” it as rewarding hackathon wins, lines of code and interesting open-source projects. added Cutting DevRel is not the same as cutting developers; he adds that “this doesn't mean there's no professional engineers around. There's 168 scientists at Input Output” (his figure).

He also says “this is not a criticism of the DevRel team we let go. They're good people and they did a lot of great work.”

3. The end of “developer count” as a metric

It meant something when humans wrote the code. Now that anyone can look like a developer, chasing the number just invites gaming.

His argument runs like this. Cardano and other chains tracked “this many developers on the chain.” When a human being was writing code, that meant a lot: a trained, experienced person choosing to spend a very finite resource on Midnight.

But this is the age of “vibe coding” and “vibe engineering,” where people with no technical knowledge or experience can use the tools to look like a developer. So developers are “no longer a scarce and finite resource”; they are “a resource that is infinite in its ability.”

Vibe coding his term

He uses it without defining it. added A phrase that spread around 2025 for building software by asking an AI in natural language and accepting what comes back if it works, rather than writing the code yourself. He distinguishes this from “vibes with skill” or “vibe engineering,” which he uses approvingly for the Midnight City team: engineers who understand the engineering, using AI to ship a great many features.

His spoken argument, drawn in two rows exactly as he gives it. “Infinite resource” is his phrase.

A naive question — if you want builders, isn't cutting the team that supports developers self-defeating?

added What follows is our explanation, not something said in the video.

The question is natural, and it is what the critics are saying. Builders write (or generate) code in the end, so the quality of tools and documentation matters to them too.

What makes his version hold together

  • The distinction he draws: what was cut is not “developers” but “the programme for growing developer count” (hackathons, lines of code, open-source rewards) and its staff. He stresses the engineers have not gone anywhere
  • If the developer-count metric is broken by AI, the return on a team whose job is to grow it falls too
  • Reverse the order: “one $10 billion venture will attract 10,000 developers,” not the other way round

How to tell from outside

Whether the Foundation's announced replacements (AI-native tools, Build Club, Night Sky) actually answer would-be builders' questions, visible in a few months in developer forums and repository activity.

What was and was not shown

Only the argument. How many people DevRel was, and what replaces it at what cost, are not said.

4. What a builder is: the MacBook Air and “show me the bridge”

Someone accountable for a product. Developers help builders but are not builders themselves, in his definition.

His definition: “A product has a product market fit. It has a consumer. It has a revenue target. It has adoption. It has a marketing plan. It has product people.” The people who build products are builders. “Developers help builders, but developers aren't builders themselves.” They can become builders, but then they are asking who the consumer of the code is and what makes it good. You can spend ten years writing technical documents, languages and protocols, he says, and “it's mental masturbation.”

His example is Steve Jobs unveiling the MacBook Air: show the competitor's thin laptop and its dimensions first, overlay the Air to show its thickest point is thinner than the competitor's thinnest, then pull it out of an envelope that had been on stage all along. “That's a builder who rallied the engineers,” who “figured out the comparables, understood what the definition of success is,” and who builds “a world, a narrative, a story.” added The envelope moment was the Macworld keynote of January 2008; his “I think it was 2008” is right.

The other example is “show me the bridge.” Someone at a bar who says “I'm a builder” gets asked “What did you build? Show me the bridge, the building, the house.” A developer can answer with test cases for financial software, CI, JSON linting, and, asked what product it is for, “I don't really know.” “Devs can be builders. Absolutely. John Carmack built Doom.”

5. Privacy is not the core differentiator

Nobody uses PGP. The private product has to be easier, cheaper and better than the non-private one, or it will not be chosen.

The most striking claim of the video, coming from the person building “the privacy chain.” Cryptographers have said “privacy, privacy, privacy” for 30 years; everyone has been able to send private email since 1991; “In the last week, be honest. How many of you have sent a PGP encrypted email?” Some use Signal, but from a GrapheneOS phone? “You say it but your consumer behavior says the exact opposite.”

From there: “If privacy is the core differentiator, it's not worth anything.” Privacy is “under the belly of it and it lives in it,” but it is not why people buy. What they buy is abstraction, smart compliance, agentic workflows: “Hey agent, I'm going to a wedding next week, find me something to wear,” with the agent choosing “the private thing by default.”

He extends the logic to principles in general. AI-alignment people telling you AI is scary will not slow it down, “because at the end of the day people are making money with the system the way it is.” “You want to get rid of coal, make solar with batteries cheaper than coal. No one's going to move… out of the goodness of their heart.” If you want privacy and self-custody to be the standard, don't lecture; “build a product where that's the standard and it's better, faster, and cheaper.”

A naive question — if privacy is not the differentiator, what is Midnight chosen for?

added What follows is our explanation, not something said in the video.

A fair question, and his answer within the video boils down to simplicity, universality and “one click” (section 9). Privacy is present but not the pitch.

Breaking it into parts

  • The PGP example shows that few people choose privacy when it costs effort, not that privacy is worthless
  • His conclusion is that the private agent wins if it is easier, cheaper and better, so privacy is not dropped but made something you get without choosing it
  • Competing on simplicity, though, means competing on the same ground as products without privacy (he himself names Meta's Muse). Why Midnight would win there is not argued in the video

How to tell from outside

Whether Midnight's products get used “without the privacy being explained,” that is, whether users pick them without noticing the privacy features. If his reasoning is right, that is what should happen.

What was and was not shown

A direction. Who provides the agent, what model it runs on and when it ships are not said, just as the “private environments to run AI in” of the previous stream (09-09) remain unexplained.

  • A financial DSL, “Moriarty,” which “resurrected Marlowe, an old idea we had,” with “the computer crunching K semantics for it right now.” added The captions say “Morardi”; his GitHub has a repository for Moriarty, a bounded financial language for Midnight (deliverables dated 2026-09-10), which is our reading. This section is dense with jargon — open a plain-language explanation

added What follows is our explanation, not something said in the video.

“Private proof-of-stake”

Proof of stake picks who makes the next block in proportion to holdings, and normally who holds how much is public. A private version lets a node prove it was chosen without revealing its holdings or identity. Crypsinous is a 2018 design for this within the Ouroboros family.

“Jolt,” “lattice,” “Nightstream”

Jolt is one way of producing a short proof that a program ran correctly (a zkVM). “Lattice-based” means resting that proof on mathematics believed to resist quantum computers; when he says in section 9 that “lattices are coming,” this is the thread. Nightstream is Midnight's own component of the same kind, and he says the two need to be compared.

“K semantics”

The K framework defines what a programming language means in a form a machine can read. Define a financial-contract language in K and you can check how a contract behaves before it runs. Marlowe was IOG's language for this around 2018; he says he is remaking it for Midnight.

An everyday analogy

A standard contract form with fixed fields and formulas instead of free-text contracts. Fewer contracts can be written, but everything that can happen is checkable in advance.

In one line

He lists three rebuilds: how Midnight reaches consensus, how it produces proofs, and how it writes contracts. All self-reported; the video offers no evidence of progress.

“Every one of these things is a star and a constellation that lights up the sky,” he says, and together they give “a path to mass adoption.” Midnight is “the hardest product we've ever built… but it's the most essential,” and “if we lose our privacy, we lose it forever.”

The second half is addressed to the Cardano community. “If Midnight fixes it, Cardano made that possible.” Without the projects built on it, Cardano “is a smart contract system to move ADA around.” Nobody had a problem with SNEK, DexHunter, SundaeSwap or Strike, he says, yet somehow with Midnight, the largest, they do, which tells people building on Midnight “you're not welcome here.”

A name, “Dreas,” comes up here; who it refers to is not explained.
  • Funders are “pathologically allergic to giving people money to create a commercial outcome.” “Oh, we can't fund that business. What if they make money?” No money for Blockfrost, none for Pogun. The sovereign wealth fund was his June 2025 proposal to convert $100 million of treasury ADA into Bitcoin and stablecoins. The basis of “400 million now” (which asset, priced when) is not given.

On the foundation his language is stronger still: “I can't do anything about the garbage foundation,” “I'm sorry about Cardano. It's my greatest mistake,” they earn the same whether ADA rises or collapses, “the board is still the board. There's no democracy. There's no oversight.” Bitcoin created trillions without the Bitcoin Foundation, and “we'll succeed despite it.” added These are his assessments of the Cardano Foundation's structure; this page has not verified them.

A naive question — wasn't Pogun voted down by DReps, not by “the foundation”?

added What follows is our explanation, not something said in the video.

Correct, and the two are easy to blur because he moves from one to the other without a break.

Breaking it into parts

  • The Pogun and Blockfrost proposals sought money from Cardano's treasury, and such proposals are decided by DReps and the Constitutional Committee under CIP-1694 governance. “You voted against it” is addressed to those voters
  • “Tweet about it” and “no oversight” are addressed to the Cardano Foundation, which is not a party to the vote (except insofar as it votes as a DRep itself)
  • The “allergic to commercial outcomes” charge is aimed, in his phrasing, at both

How to tell from outside

The vote breakdown of the rejected proposals (yes, no, abstain, and how the large DReps voted) is on chain for anyone to check. The foundation's conduct is read from its annual reports. They are different things.

What was and was not shown

9. “Click the button”: the iPhone and the phone in the bathroom

Apple spent 20 years arguing for the Mac and the share never moved; the iPhone changed it at once. Hide the complexity.

His closing argument is simplicity. Apple spent 20 years trying to convince people macOS was better and “the percentage distribution of Windows versus Mac didn't move”; “then they built the iPhone and it was just push the button and it destroyed the entire Windows monopoly.” Don't ask people to migrate; give them an experience so simple the old one looks antiquated. Hence the phone on the toilet, and the phone as “part of your body now, like your hand or your ears.”

Applied to Midnight: “Bitcoin DeFi, click the toggle, get 5%. RealFi, click the toggle. His “front and back,” drawn as he gave it. The back-end components are in the order he said them; where each is used in Midnight is not explained.
